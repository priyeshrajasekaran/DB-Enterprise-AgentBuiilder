PRFAQ — Enterprise Agent Builder Platform

The end state, written first as the internal announcement we intend to send

Any team can now ship a governed agent on shared rails, and GTM shipped the first one
For the CIO and the GTM, Finance, HR, and Product teams

The Enterprise Agent Builder Platform is now live. Any domain team within Databricks can start from a golden-path template and have an agent deployed in production in as little as four weeks, with built-in data access and governance, evaluation rails, deployment, tracing, and cost tracking. Bricksters can now spend their time on the parts that are actually their own: the tools the agent calls, the prompt, and what a good answer looks like in their domain. The first agent built this way is a GTM account-research and call-prep assistant. It went from idea to production within four weeks on the shared rails.

The platform is a thin, opinionated layer on top of Agent Bricks that standardizes how access is governed, how quality is gated, and how agents are cataloged. That gives teams a paved road to ship faster, and with confidence.

Core principle: teams own their domain and agentic outcomes; the platform owns the substrate underneath.

FAQ

Why build with GTM first?

GTM exercises the hard parts of the substrate: governed CRM access, retrieval over messy account history, and evaluation of open-ended output. Increasing the velocity of shipping agentic experiences in GTM will, in our assessment, have the most tangible impact on Databricks' revenue growth and the success of our customers.

We debated Finance and HR, and decided to sequence them as follow-up use cases. They carry a higher governance burden and a lower tolerance for a wrong answer, so they benefit most from onboarding after the governance and eval patterns are proven.

What does the platform give me that I don't get by building it myself?

The split is: you bring the three things that are actually yours, and the platform owns everything underneath them.

Your model

Point at any model served through the platform's routing layer. Switching models is a config change, not a code change.

Your prompt

Author it inline or reference a versioned prompt. We scan for inline secrets before anything publishes.

Your tools

Register what the agent calls: an MCP server, an OpenAPI spec, or a plain HTTP endpoint with a schema. Access is scoped to the caller through Unity Catalog.

What the platform owns, so no team has to build it twice:

  • The agent skeleton and graph topology. One standard shape (the ResponsesAgent contract) so every agent is traceable, testable, and deployable the same way.
  • Governed data and tool access through Unity Catalog, with per-tool auth and access enforced on behalf of the calling user.
  • The eval pipeline, hooked up and waiting for the team's golden dataset and rubric.
  • Draft, publish, invoke. Test against a draft and iterate without republishing; ship when it holds up.
  • Version history with one-click rollback, so a bad prompt change can be reverted without a redeploy.
  • Execution tracing — the full tool-call tree for a run, including inputs, outputs, and latency per step.
  • Deploy, CI, and cost tracking on by default, scoped per team.

Everything on this list is either a one-way door that is expensive to retrofit later, or something every agent needs. A team building bespoke builds all of it themselves, and then owns it.

What did you deliberately not build?

We deferred these to avoid building shared abstractions from a sample of one.

Cut Why it's out of V1 Instead
Custom model training / fine-tuning Foundation models are sufficient for starter use cases. Foundation Model APIs via model routing.
Shared tool catalog A catalog at n=1 is a sub-optimal abstraction. Governed registration pathway; the catalog organically emerges from reuse.
Bespoke frontend framework Commoditized; not our differentiation. Databricks Apps (Streamlit or React).
Streaming-first / real-time Complexity the first use cases don't need. Request/response with human review.
Visual / drag-and-drop agent builder Premature; our users are Python-fluent engineers. Config-driven registration covers them without a builder UI. Declarative config + docs.
Multi-agent orchestration Reuse pattern unknown at one agent. Simple in-agent steps; platformize after the additional use cases.

How does the platform avoid becoming a bottleneck as more teams adopt it?

Teams can build off the suggested path. We are targeting earned adoption, not mandated adoption. We enforce two binding constraints and nothing else:

  • Adoption tiers — standardized checkpoints to graduate an agent across environments.
  • The agent registry — every deployed agent is cataloged, on or off the path.

We will also monitor why teams go off-path. Understanding the rationale behind those decisions gives us a field signal on how to prioritize the platform backlog.

What are the adoption tiers, and the governance checkpoint at each?

Tier What it is Checkpoint to advance
Tier 1 — Sandbox / internal-assist Human-in-the-loop, internal users, drafts only. Automated checks only: passing eval on the team's golden dataset, catalog registration, UC access scoped to caller.
Tier 2 — Internal production Runs in a team's real workflow, still internal.
  • Named owner and on-call
  • Gateway rate limits and PII guardrails on; cost budget set
  • Platform reviews eval results and data scope
Tier 3 — External / regulated Touches customers, finances, or regulated data.
  • Security, legal, and red-team sign-off
  • Full audit trail
  • Human-in-the-loop or an approved autonomy policy

Registration is required at all three, and the automated Tier 1 checks apply on or off the golden path.

Learning Plan

Success Metrics report whether the platform works. This plan is what the first two builds have to teach us. At n=1 most of the claims in this document are assumptions, so each row names what we are testing, the metric that settles it, and what we do with the answer.

Learning priorities

Ordered by how much launch risk they carry:

  1. Speed. Is the golden path faster than a team building it themselves? A GTM build that runs to twelve weeks invalidates the case for the platform.
  2. Governance throughput. Does a standard blueprint turn security review into a pattern check? Review time is the likeliest place the four-week target slips without us noticing.
  3. Self-serve. Can a second team ship without us in the room? If every build needs an embedded platform engineer, we do not scale past a handful of teams.
  4. Right abstractions. Did we platformize the correct things at n=1, or did we guess? Measured by what the second team keeps versus rewrites.

The problem

Customer problem and ideal state

Customer problem statement

I am: An engineer on a domain team — GTM, Finance, HR, or Product — handed a real agentic use case and a quarter to ship it.

I am trying to: Get a governed agent into production that my team can rely on in a real workflow.

But: The quarter goes to plumbing rather than to the agent. Security review alone stretches a weeks-long build into months.

Because: There is no paved road. I build my own eval harness, my own path to a governed data source, my own deployment, and I walk security through a system they have never seen before — as do the three teams building next to me.

The cost compounds from the fourth agent to the twentieth. The same plumbing gets rebuilt, it drifts, and nobody can answer which agents see which data, what they cost, or whether they still pass their own quality bar.

Hypothesis

If we: Give every domain team a governed scaffold on day one, with data access and security review pre-paid by a blueprint rather than negotiated per agent.

Then: Teams will spend the four weeks on the parts that are actually theirs — the tools the agent calls, the prompt, and what a good answer looks like in their domain — and ship in weeks rather than quarters.

Which we will measure by: Time from idea to a Tier 1 production deploy for a standard agent, and the share of that time spent on domain work versus plumbing.

Success metric for this will be: The GTM agent reaches production in under four weeks, and a second team repeats it without an embedded platform engineer.

Ideal state

Every deployed agent is registered, so the platform team can answer who owns what, what it touches, and what it costs — without asking around.

Core principle: teams own their domain and agentic outcomes; the platform owns the substrate underneath.

V1 scope

Key features

V1 is the platform plus the one agent that proves it. We platformize two kinds of things: one-way doors that are expensive to retrofit across many agents, and things every agent needs that are nearly free to generalize. Everything else stays bespoke until a second agent shows the real reuse pattern.

What the platform owns

Feature What it is Why platform
Golden-path template Runnable agent scaffold on day one: ResponsesAgent, eval hook, and deploy config in an Asset Bundle with CI. Universal and near-free to generalize.
Governed data & tool access Unity Catalog as the permission spine, with on-behalf-of identity so an agent only touches what the caller can. We own the pathway to register a tool, not the tools. One-way door. Collapses the multi-week security path into a pattern check.
Eval pipeline Four gated stages: content quality against the team's golden dataset, latency against the tool budget, regression pass on every change, and RAI verification (PII, safety, prompt-injection). Teams own the rubric, dataset, and thresholds; the platform owns the machinery and the RAI floor. One-way door and universal. The machinery generalizes; "what good means" is domain knowledge we should not fake.
One-command deploy Databricks Apps as the single target, Lakebase for memory, rollback supported. Universal. Prevents notebook-versus-app drift.
Observability + cost MLflow tracing, usage tables, per-agent token cost by default, budget policy attached at deploy. One-way door for lineage, universal for the rest.
Agent catalog Every agent registers at deploy with owner, tier, data scope, and cost. Required at every tier. One-way door. The only way to keep sprawl visible.

What the domain team owns

Which tools the agent calls and what they do, the prompt and domain logic, the golden dataset and rubric that define a good answer, and any multi-step logic inside the agent. We ship a safe way to plug a tool in, not a catalog of tools — at one agent we do not know which tools generalize.

Runbook

Deploying an agent in four weeks

The target is under four weeks. It holds because the two items that used to eat the quarter — governed data access and security review — are pre-paid by the blueprint. What remains on the clock:

Week 1
Scaffold & golden dataset

Scaffold from the template (runnable day one), confirm UC access, start the golden dataset — the team writes examples of a good answer from day one, not after the agent exists.

Team doesScaffolds the project, confirms the data it needs is reachable, writes the first golden-dataset examples.
Platform doesTemplate, UC access pathway, eval harness already wired.
Done whenThe scaffold runs end to end against a sample tool and the golden dataset has real examples in it.
Week 2
Tools and prompt

Register tools through the governed pathway; build domain logic against the trace.

Team doesRegisters the tools the agent calls, writes the prompt, iterates against the trace.
Platform doesGoverned registration pathway, tracing on by default.
Done whenThe agent calls its real tools with access scoped to the caller.
Week 3
Eval iterations

Run the golden dataset against the gate, fix what fails, tighten the rubric. This is where most of the quality work lands, and the schedule accounts for it.

Team doesRuns evals, fixes failures, sharpens the rubric as they learn what good means.
Platform doesEval machinery and the RAI floor (PII, safety, prompt-injection).
Done whenThe golden dataset passes at the team's threshold.
Week 4
Blueprint check & deploy

Blueprint check, deploy, rollout behind human review. Security verifies a pattern instead of investigating a system; registration happens at deploy.

Team doesRuns the blueprint check, deploys, turns the agent on behind human review.
Platform doesPre-approved blueprint, one-command deploy, catalog registration.
Done whenAgent is live at Tier 1, registered in the catalog, with a named owner.

A design target, not a schedule with a track record — the GTM build tests it, and its retro rewrites this table before the second team starts.
